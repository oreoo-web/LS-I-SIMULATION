Laboratory Input Calculations & Measurement Notes

Swelling ratio (%) = [(Wₜ − W₀) / W₀] × 100

W₀: weigh the lyophilized dry hydrogel before immersion. Wₜ: after the chosen swelling time, remove the sample, blot surface liquid, and weigh it. The page computes the ratio from those two entries. Use PBS at 37°C, a stated time point (or equilibrium), and replicate measurements. Sources: rbaf052 and PMC11986482.

pH = calibrated pH-meter reading (no arithmetic conversion)

Use a calibrated pH meter on a consistently prepared hydrogel solution/extract, record the dilution, temperature and time point, and report replicate readings (mean ± SD). The cited study measured 1 g lyophilized hydrogel dissolved in 5 mL PBS and read the solution in triplicate. Source: PMC11337927. Enter the measured pH; do not substitute the paper's example values.

IC₅₀ = concentration producing 50% DPPH radical scavenging (µg/mL)

Measure DPPH response over a concentration series, calculate scavenging at each concentration, then estimate the 50% point from the concentration–response curve (preferably nonlinear dose-response fit; report the fit and replicates). A single absorbance or concentration is insufficient. The cited paper derives IC₅₀ from its DPPH assay; this is an antioxidant IC₅₀, not a cell-toxicity IC₅₀. Source: Scientific Reports s41598-025-03555-x.

DPPH scavenging (%) = [(Ablank − Asample) / Ablank] × 100

Use the DPPH control/blank absorbance and sample-plus-DPPH absorbance at 517 nm after the specified dark incubation. Enter the resulting percentage. Source: PMC9909831.

Cumulative release (%) = cumulative mass released by t / initially loaded mass × 100

Quantify released material at each time point using a calibration curve and account for withdrawn/replaced aliquots; divide cumulative released mass by initial loaded mass. Enter the percentage at a stated time point; it cannot be derived without loaded mass and release measurements. Source: PMC10975535.

Diffusion area (cm²) = measured exposed membrane / hydrogel contact area

Use the actual exposed cross-sectional/contact area from the diffusion-cell geometry (for a circular opening, πr² with r in cm). Do not use the whole hydrogel surface unless that entire surface is exposed. Source: PMC9866939.

Cell migration / scratch closure (%) = [(A₀ − Aₜ) / A₀] × 100

Measure the cell-free scratch area in calibrated images at time zero (A₀) and the selected later time (Aₜ), e.g. using ImageJ. The page uses these areas to compute percent closure. Keep units and imaging scale consistent. Sources: PMC11492349 and rbad023.

Toxicity (%) = [(Number of dead brine shrimp) / (Total number of brine shrimp)] × 100

Use the number of dead brine shrimp and the total number of brine shrimp used in the assay. If survival percentage is used: Toxicity (%) = 100 − Survival (%).

Scope: Laboratory-derived swelling, pH, IC₅₀, DPPH, release, migration and MTT toxicity values feed corresponding simulation modifiers shown in the mapping table. Diffusion area supports flux calculation only when paired with permeated amount and time data; those measurements are not collected here. Model coefficients remain assumptions and require calibration against independent wound-healing data before predictive accuracy can be claimed. Literature values are method references, not default measurements for these formulations.

Integrated lab-to-simulation calculation: swelling = (Wt−W0)/W0×100; antioxidant index = 0.75×DPPH fraction + 0.25×IC50 index; exposure = release fraction; hydration factor = clamp(0.96 + 0.08×swelling fraction, 0.90, 1.08); pH factor = clamp(1−0.06×|pH−7.4|, 0.80, 1); survival factor = 1−toxicity fraction. These factors modify simulated migration, proliferation and the treatment closure ceiling. Scratch-area measurements add a measured-closure modifier when both areas are entered. Diffusion area is recorded, but cannot change model exposure by itself: permeated amount and time (and loaded dose) are needed to calculate flux. Coefficients and mappings are assumptions, not validated biological laws; combining them does not establish predictive accuracy. Cell proliferation uses the entered doubling time.

How to Use the Platform

  1. Complete laboratory characterization first and enter the actual measurements.
  2. Measure wound area from experimental images at consistent time points.
  3. Enter the measured wound areas under Measured Wound-Healing Data.
  4. Calculate wound closure using the area-based formula.
  5. Use the 3D simulation to visualize modeled migration/proliferation behavior.
  6. Run three independent computational runs for each group.
  7. Report measured data separately from simulated data.

Source-to-Model Mapping

  • Toxicity: Toxicity (%) = (number of dead brine shrimp / total number of brine shrimp) × 100; if survival percentage is used, Toxicity (%) = 100 − Survival (%).
  • Cell migration: the cited chitosan/gelatin/PEDOT:PSS Figure 11 is a scratch assay; use measured closure over time, not a universal speed constant. If converting to edge speed, report the measurement definition and units (µm/h).
  • Diffusion: flux J from cumulative permeation per unit area over time and Kp = J/Cd from Thakkar et al. (2016). citeturn1search4
  • DPPH: [(Ak−As)/Ak]×100 from Zhang et al. (2021). citeturn1search2
  • Initial wound: 2 × 2 cm excision from the Immortelle diabetic-wound study (20 mm width used as the linear model gap).
  • Starting cells: the cited AgNP-CS/PVA study seeded 100,000 cells/mL × 1 mL for its scratch assay; the platform uses up to 1000 representative agents, not literal cells.
  • Reference antioxidant IC₅₀: approximately 7.69 µg/mL from the Tridax patch study; this DPPH IC₅₀ is not a cytotoxicity IC₅₀.
  • Doubling time: calculate DT = t × ln(2) / ln(Nt/N0) from the cited fibroblast study; its result varies by passage and cell type, so 24 h is only a starting model assumption.
  • Wound closure: area-based closure = [(A0−At)/A0]×100 from the Mupirocin–KGF and Immortelle wound studies.

Scope and interpretation

This platform is intended as an in-silico wound-healing stage for comparing modeled wound-closure behavior. It estimates wound-gap closure based on the entered laboratory values and model assumptions; it does not represent every biological process, such as inflammation, extracellular-matrix remodeling, angiogenesis, or re-epithelialization. The model should be calibrated and validated against suitable reference data before its predictions are treated as reliable.
